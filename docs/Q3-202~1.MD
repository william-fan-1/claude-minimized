# Q3 2026 Sector Research Briefing
**For:** Explaining Markets team · **As of:** August 3, 2026 · **Covers:** the regime our agent will predict into, Aug 10 – Oct 2

> **Why this exists.** Company history tells us how hard a stock reacts. This briefing tells us *what the market is currently rewarding and punishing* — which no amount of company history captures, because it's this quarter's news. These findings decay. Re-run this research at the September refinement pass.

---

## 1. The macro backdrop — read this first, it drives everything below

**An oil shock is the organizing fact of this market.** The Iran conflict pushed WTI up **77% year-to-date**, settling above $90/bbl on 43 of the 51 trading days since it began. That flows straight into everything else: input costs, inflation prints, and Fed expectations.

**The rate path has flipped and is genuinely contested.** Oil-driven inflation caused a hawkish repricing — at one point money markets priced roughly **50% odds of a July rate hike**, a sharp reversal from expected cuts. Other commentary still frames a September cut as live. Treat the rate path as **two-sided and unstable**, not as a directional view. What matters for us: rate-sensitive commentary on calls will move stocks more than usual.

**Tariffs are still transmitting into margins.** The ISM Prices Index spiked to **78.3 in March** — highest since June 2022 — then eased to **73.0 by June**. Still elevated. Manufacturing itself is healthy: **July ISM PMI 55.6**, six straight months of expansion.

**Aggregate earnings are strong.** Consensus S&P 500 growth around **23.6%**, and estimates were revised *up* 3.4% during the last season — unusual, since they typically fall 2–3%.

### The single most important calibration fact

**Dispersion is at a six-year high and the market is punishing misses harder than usual.**

The DSPX index (30-day expected dispersion within the S&P) hit **47%, a six-year high** — above even the April "Liberation Day" peak when VIX touched 60. It has broken its normal pattern of falling after earnings season. Meanwhile VIX sits near its median at ~18. That combination means index-level calm masking violent *single-stock* and *sector* divergence.

Commentary is explicit that beats are being rewarded with meaningful outperformance while **even minor misses trigger severe selloffs**.

**What this means for our predictions:** the tails are fatter than normal this quarter. Our anti-clustering instruction isn't just a scoring trick — it's descriptively correct for this regime. And there's a genuine **negative asymmetry**: we should be quicker to reach for 0.05–0.15 on a miss than to reach for 0.85–0.95 on a beat.

## 2. Business Equipment (tech, semis, software) — largest bucket, ~20% of events

This industry has **three sub-regimes pulling in different directions.** Treating it as one sector is the biggest modelling error available to us.

### 2a. Hyperscalers / AI capex — capex is now a liability, not a growth signal

This is the defining dynamic of the quarter, and it's exactly the case William raised.

On **July 28, Alphabet reported accelerating cloud growth and got panned** — because it hiked its 2026 capex forecast. Shares of **Amazon, Meta and Microsoft fell in sympathy.** Consensus 2026 capex across the five big hyperscalers rose from **$467bn to $533bn** over the course of the season, and investors reacted badly.

The market's question has shifted from "are you investing in AI?" to "**can you show the revenue that justifies it, and how are you funding it?**" Return dispersion within the group is widening based on exactly that, and on the leverage used to fund the spend.

**Rule implication:** raised capex without matching revenue visibility is now a *negative*, reversing the 2023–25 pattern where AI investment was rewarded on announcement.

### 2b. Semiconductors / memory — the strongest tape in the market

Global semi revenue is projected above **$1.3 trillion in 2026**, the strongest growth in two decades. Memory is the epicentre — "memflation": **DRAM prices +125%, NAND +234%** in 2026, with no meaningful relief expected until late 2027. Supply constraints across HBM, DRAM and NAND persist beyond 2026.

Micron is up ~**104% over six months and 512% over twelve**; SK Hynix similar. Agentic AI is also shifting inference demand toward CPUs, which lifted Intel, AMD and Arm.

But the bar is correspondingly high. **Broadcom guided Q3 AI chip revenue to $16bn against a $17.2bn consensus and fell 14% in a day.** In a sector priced for acceleration, an in-line guide is a miss.

**Rule implication:** for semis, guidance *magnitude* relative to an elevated bar matters more than the reported quarter. Also watch which side of memflation a company sits on — memory makers benefit, device and hardware assemblers eat the cost.

### 2c. Software / SaaS — a structural de-rating, not a cyclical wobble

The "SaaSpocalypse." Software stocks have lost **over $1 trillion in 2026**, with roughly **$285bn wiped in early 2026** alone. The mechanism is unusual and worth understanding: AI is working *too well*. Customers equipped with AI agents accomplish more with fewer licenses, so they're **cutting seats rather than adding them**.

Per-seat pricing has collapsed from **21% to 15%** of SaaS companies in twelve months, while hybrid models jumped **27% to 41%**.

**Rule implication:** for seat-based software, net revenue retention and seat counts are now the critical line items, and any hint of seat compression outweighs a headline beat. Conversely, credible migration to usage-based pricing is a positive the market is actively looking for.

## 3. Health — second largest bucket, ~17% of events

**The pricing overhang has partly cleared.** The sector recovered over the past two to three months as most large drugmakers signed drug-pricing agreements with the administration. Eli Lilly and Novo Nordisk agreed to lower GLP-1 costs for government programs and direct-to-consumer channels.

**But the cost is quantified:** US GLP-1 price reductions are expected to reduce obesity-drug revenues by **$2–3bn this year**. So volume growth and price concession are now running against each other, and calls will be scrutinised for which is winning.

GLP-1 remains the sector's engine — global obesity drug sales projected at **$39.4bn in 2026**, potentially above $100bn by decade's end; Lilly passed a **$1 trillion** market cap in late 2025.

For biotech, profitability is improving structurally: only ~20% of the S&P Biotechnology index was profitable across 2017–24, a figure that could nearly **double to 38% by 2027**. Investors are prioritising **durable pipelines and clear catalysts** — obesity, cell and gene therapy, inflammation, rare disease, neuroscience, next-gen oncology.

**Rule implication:** healthcare splits sharply. For large pharma, the pricing/volume trade and pipeline depth dominate. For pre-profit biotech, a single catalyst or readout swamps the financials entirely — these are the highest-variance names in the sample and belong in the tails.

## 4. Finance — third largest bucket, ~16% of events

**Margins are compressing.** Net interest margins fell from **3.30% in Q4 2025 to 3.22% in Q1 2026** — the first decline in net interest income since mid-2024. Loan yields dropped from 6.75% to 6.51%, having peaked at 7.13% in late 2024.

**Credit is benign but with specific pockets of stress.** Commercial credit concerns are easing across commercial real estate and private credit, and consumer delinquencies are falling — *except* in **lower-income and lower-FICO segments**, which remain stressed. That distinction matters: a bank with subprime consumer exposure is a different story from one without.

**Where the market is looking**, in priority order: management commentary on net interest income and **deposit pricing dynamics**; credit quality metrics and **loan-loss provision guidance**; and **capital markets / investment banking pipelines**.

Bank stocks were among the top winners of the hawkish rate repricing.

**Rule implication:** deposit costs and NII trajectory outrank headline EPS. Provision *builds* are read as forward-looking warnings even when the current quarter is clean. And per the paper's own finding, buybacks in financials are routine capital maintenance — not catalysts.

## 5. Shops (retail / consumer) — the weakest fundamental picture

**The consumer is visibly cracking.** US consumer spending has fallen by its widest margin in four years, driven by tariff shocks, a softening labour market, and a pronounced flight to value.

**Walmart guided below expectations** on slower sales and profit growth, and noted that households earning around **$50,000 a year are making smaller purchases and timing trips to paycheck schedules** — an unusually specific distress signal. Guidance from Walmart, Ross and TJX collectively points to a tougher road ahead. e.l.f. Beauty's CEO said plainly that the "consumer is suffering," and is rolling back tariff-driven price increases.

**Prices stay sticky.** Durable goods — electronics, toys, tools, small appliances — are expected to rise **4.5% in 2026**, and increases already taken are not expected to reverse.

There's also a data-quality warning: Q1 2026 retail strength was likely flattered by **tax refunds and deeper buy-now-pay-later usage**, masking underlying weakness. Comparisons against that quarter will look artificially hard.

**Rule implication:** in retail, guidance and traffic/basket commentary dominate. Price-led revenue growth with declining volumes should be read as a warning, not strength — and this sector is where that pattern is most likely to appear.

## 6. Manufacturing / Industrials

Activity is genuinely healthy — **ISM PMI 55.6 in July**, expansion for six consecutive months, with the broader economy growing for 21 straight months. The problem is the **margin squeeze**, driven by tariff transmission lag and a spike in raw-material costs concentrated in metals and energy.

There are clear relative winners: domestic steel producers such as Nucor and Steel Dynamics can push through their own price increases in a protectionist environment.

**Rule implication:** demand commentary is not the swing factor here — **input-cost pass-through and margin guidance are**. A volume beat with margin compression should read negative.

---

## 7. Utilities

The data-centre load story is real: US data-centre electricity demand is projected to **triple between 2024 and 2030**, rising from 3% to 10% of total US demand, adding roughly **125 GW** and pushing overall demand growth to a **4.1% CAGR** through 2030. IT load could double from ~80 GW in 2025 to ~150 GW by 2028. AEP reaffirmed 7–9% growth guidance through 2030.

**But the forecasts are being trimmed and politics is intruding.** The EIA **cut its 2026 power generation forecast by more than a percentage point**, and slashed expected ERCOT growth from 15.7% to 9.6% for 2026. **Affordability is looming large** as a political issue, and utilities are visibly divided on how aggressively to court data-centre load.

**Rule implication:** a data-centre contract announcement is no longer an automatic positive — the market now asks who bears the cost and whether regulators will allow recovery. Rate-case outcomes and affordability commentary are the swing factors. Note also the paper's finding that in utilities and telecom, **churn and free cash flow outweigh subscriber/volume growth**.

---

## 8. Telecom

The sector "finally looks healthier," and the Q2 2026 prints show precisely what the market rewards.

**Verizon** delivered 184k postpaid phone net adds against 106k consensus, consumer postpaid churn down 6bps to **0.84%**, and first-half free cash flow of **$10.2bn versus $8.8bn** a year earlier. **AT&T rose over 3%** on July 22, with 432k postpaid net adds and — critically — **42% of home internet customers now also taking wireless**, validating the convergence thesis and lowering churn.

**T-Mobile is the instructive counter-example:** it beat EPS by more than 15% and raised free cash flow guidance, but total postpaid net account additions of 277k were **down 13% year over year**. Beat plus decelerating subscriber momentum is a materially weaker setup than the headline suggests.

**Rule implication:** churn direction, free cash flow, and convergence metrics outrank gross subscriber adds. Decelerating adds alongside an EPS beat is a caution signal.

---

## 9. Energy

Q1 E&P profits **doubled** on the oil spike. And yet — the sector held the line on spending. The 36 tracked companies set 2026 capital investment at **$59.1bn, down 5%** from $62.5bn in 2025. Commentary is explicit that betting on a capex surge to chase the windfall "would have been a losing bet."

Midstream continues to compound quietly on fee-based, inflation-protected contracts, with cash flows growing through tariff threats, geopolitical flare-ups and OPEC+ uncertainty.

**Rule implication:** this is the cleanest inverse of the tech capex rule. In energy, **capital discipline is rewarded and a capex increase is punished**, even when cash flows justify it. Distributions and buybacks funded from windfall cash flow are read positively.

---

## 10. Cross-cutting takeaways for the prompt

1. **Fatter tails than normal.** Dispersion at a six-year high justifies more aggressive use of the 0–1 range.
2. **Negative asymmetry.** Misses are punished harder than beats are rewarded this quarter. Reach for the bottom decile faster than the top.
3. **Capex means opposite things in different sectors.** Punished in tech, rewarded-when-restrained in energy, scrutinised for cost recovery in utilities. A single global "capex" rule would be actively wrong.
4. **Guidance dominates everywhere**, and the bar is elevated because estimates were revised *up* into this season.
5. **Oil and rates are the shared shock.** Any company commenting on input costs, fuel, freight or rate sensitivity is being listened to more closely than usual.
